## Full disclosure

### Project ownership and direction

Shivam Gupta is the project creator. He defined the objective: an end-to-end healthcare innovation with a working product, clear presentation, rigorous testing, and a credible commercial path. OpenAI Codex provided substantial assistance in research, concept development, software implementation, testing, and drafting the submission and presentation materials.

Do not add claims of manual coding, clinical review, customer interviews, fieldwork, or personal testing unless Shivam actually performs those activities. The AI assistant is a development tool, not an eligible human teammate.

### Evidence and data

The default demonstration uses synthetic recall and inventory records. These are illustrative test fixtures, not records from a clinic, manufacturer, patient, or deployment. Signed-in evaluation workspaces are stored server-side in Cloudflare D1 and scoped to the individual account through Sites authentication. No local-only storage claim is made. Any demonstration counts describe those fixtures only.

Public FDA material informs the description of recall-response work. The historical example below is a reference, not a claim that Lotline monitors every recall, a live handling instruction, or an endorsement:

### Software

Lotline uses third-party open-source software. The repository's package manifest, lockfile, and license files identify the actual dependencies. Retain required third-party notices and licenses. Do not describe libraries, components, or generated scaffolding as original inventions.

### Limits of the current project

The hackathon build demonstrates a recall-response workflow. It has not established clinical effectiveness, regulatory compliance, customer demand, or financial performance. Pilot design, pricing, distribution routes, and intended benefits are proposals to test. A response receipt records the software workflow and reported actions; it is not independent certification of physical stock handling.
